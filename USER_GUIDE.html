0. How Arkon fits together

Everything in Arkon is connected: what you enter in one place is used automatically in the next. Knowing the chain helps you understand why a setting matters and where a number comes from.

The chain, step by step:

  1. Setup data comes first.

    • Settings and your company: the VAT rate and currency are used in every price calculation; the company's name and NIPT print on every invoice and fill the VAT books and the official statements.

    • Products (with their prices and class), suppliers (suppliers) and clients: every order line needs a product, and every order needs a supplier or a client.

  2. A purchase adds stock and sets its cost.

    • Each product's average cost comes from what you actually paid.
    • The purchase also creates a debt to the supplier until it's paid.

  3. A sale takes stock out.

    • Cost and profit: the sale uses the average cost to know its cost of goods, so profit = sale value − cost.

    • Client debt: it creates a debt from the client until it's paid.

    • Offers don't touch stock until they're turned into a sale.

  4. Payments settle those debts. Each order shows Paid / Partly paid / Unpaid. With Cash & Bank on, every payment also moves money into or out of a cash register or bank account.

  5. Accounting is built from all of the above.

    • Automatic entries: sales, purchases, returns, payments, Cash & Bank entries, payroll and depreciation each become journal entries automatically. You don't type them twice.

    • What's built from them: the VAT books, the income statement and balance sheet, the official statements and Tax due.

What this means in practice:

  • Missing early data spreads: a mistake or gap early in the chain shows up later. For example, a product with no buy price and no purchases has a cost of 0, so its sales show 100 % profit.

  • Fix at the source: correct the purchase, sale or payment, not the report. Reports and accounting are recalculated from the source.

  • Optional modules plug into the chain where they belong (see the table below). With a module off, its part of the chain is simply skipped.

The account menu (top right)

Click your initials and username to open it:

  • Profile card: your full name, username and role (Administrator, User or Accountant).

  • Last login: when you last logged in before this session. If you don't recognise the time, tell an administrator.

  • Language: the 🇦🇱 / 🇬🇧 buttons. The choice is remembered on that computer.

  • Change password: you need your current password. After the change you're logged out and log in again with the new one.

  • Log out.

Light and dark theme

The round sun button left of your username switches to the dark theme; it then shows a moon, which switches back. The choice is remembered in that browser. Printed pages always use a white background.

Roles and access

There are three roles:

  • Administrator: everything, including Administration (users, settings, backup, audit log, registers).

  • User: purchases, sales, offers and their invoices (their own orders).

  • Accountant: view only. Sees everyone's orders, reports, statistics, unpaid amounts, Cash & Bank, tax due, payroll, assets, production and cost centres, and can export and print, but can't change anything.

Access per user (Administration → Users → a user → Access): every area can be set to Role default, No access, View only or Full access. For example, a salesperson can also get View only on Cash & Bank, or an accountant Full access to Payroll.

  • Enforced on the server: without View access, a page answers "no access"; without Full access, nothing can be saved.

  • Modules: a module switched off in Settings stays hidden for everyone.

  • Administrators always have full access.

How it works:

  • Same as main area (the default): the sub-area follows its main area, so nothing changes until you choose otherwise.

  • None / View / Full sets a level for just that part. Examples:

    • a payroll clerk with Full on Payroll but View on Income tax (TAP) and contributions;

    • a cashier with Full on Sales but None on Payments received;

    • an assistant accountant without Update entries and year-end closing.

  • A sub-area's own level stays even if its main area is changed later.

  • The menu and buttons follow it: a user without access doesn't see that page's tab or buttons. View shows the page without the buttons that change things.

  • Switched-off modules keep their sub-areas off too.

Company-wide areas (Reports, Statistics, Accounting, Cash & Bank, client and supplier statements) show company totals. Use the area access levels above to decide who can open them.

Set things up in this order. Each step uses the ones before it, so skipping ahead means coming back later to fix data. The Why now column says what depends on each step.

# Step Where Why now

2 Your company Administration → Companies It's the seller on sales and the buyer on purchases; its NIPT and address print on invoices, VAT books and statements
3 Modules Settings → Modules Switch on what you'll use now; each adds its menus and fields (see chapter 0)
4 Users, roles and access Administration → Users Every order remembers who created it; access decides who sees what
5 Product classes Administration → Product classes Products are assigned to a class; client discounts work per class
6 Products Stock → Products Every order line needs a product
7 Suppliers and clients Administration → Suppliers / Clients Every purchase needs a supplier, every sale a client
8 Shops and warehouses Stock → Locations Only with that module: users and stock belong to a location
9 Cash & Bank accounts Cash & Bank → Accounts Only with that module: payments need an account to go into
10 Exchange rates Cash & Bank → Exchange rates Only with Multi-currency: EUR/USD orders need the day's rate

1. Settings

Administration → Settings:

  • Money and invoices:

    • Currency: e.g. ALL. It's the base currency of all amounts, reports and accounting. Set it once before the first order and don't change it later.

    • VAT %: e.g. 20. The normal VAT rate for every sale and purchase. Clients with their own VAT (e.g. 0 % for exports) are set on the client.

    • Invoice colour and logo: used on invoices, credit notes and purchase orders.

  • Payment terms and offers:

    • Payment due after (days): the default due date of sales and purchases (default 30). It drives Overdue, the aged receivables and payment reminders.

    • Offers valid for (days): how long a new offer stays open (default 15).

  • Invoice language: Albanian or English. Invoices and journal descriptions are written in it.

2. Your company

Administration → Companies. Fill in name, NIPT, address, email and phone.

  • Where it's used: invoices, the tax office VAT books and the official financial statements.

  • With Accounting: also fill in Settings → Company details for the financial statements (legal form, date founded, main activity) for the statements' cover page.

3. Modules

Settings → Modules. Everything optional is off until you switch it on, and its menus only appear then. Start with what you need now; you can switch modules on later without losing anything. Chapter 0 shows what each one needs.

4. Users, roles and access

Administration → Users. Create an account for each person:

  • Role:

    • Administrator: everything.
    • User: sells and buys.
    • Accountant: views everything, full access to accounting.

  • Access: per area and sub-area, None / View / Full (see Roles and access).

  • Records this user sees: all, or only their own and those shared with them.

  • Works at: their shop, with Shops and warehouses.

Do this before daily work starts: every order permanently records who created it, and that drives the own-records view, sharing and the statistics per user.

5. Product classes and discounts

Administration → Product classes and discounts:

  • Class: a group of products, e.g. "Cables" or "Lighting". A product belongs to one class.

  • Client discount: a client gets a percentage off every product in a class, e.g. Beni Shehu, 5 % off Cables. Sales and offers to that client apply it automatically.

Create the classes before the products so each product can be given its class right away.

6. Products

Stock → Products. Many products? Use Administration → Import from Excel → Products instead of typing them one by one (see chapter 12).

Field Meaning
Name Must be unique. Stock is counted by this name (see section 8)
Barcode / product code Optional, unique. Your own product code (e.g. KB-325) or the barcode. Shown on order lines, shelves and pick lists; scanning or typing it on an order page picks the product
Unit pcs, kg, m, box…
Buy price The usual purchase price. Used as the default on purchases
Sale price The price on sales and offers, before discount
Quantity limit The minimum stock to keep. 0 = no limit (see below)
Discount class The group used for client discounts (see Product classes)

Quantity limit example: the limit is 5 and 7 are in stock. Selling 3 would leave 4, which is below 5, so the order page warns you. You can still sell; it's only a warning. The product also appears in the warehouse For supply report and in To restock on Home.

  • Name: choose names carefully. Stock is counted by name, so renaming a product starts its stock from zero (see chapter 8).

  • Buy price: the cost of a product until it's purchased in Arkon. After the first purchase, the real average cost is used.

  • Tracking (with Batches and serial numbers): choose before the first purchase of the product. Stock bought before tracking has no batch or serial numbers.

7. Suppliers and clients

Administration → Suppliers and Clients. Lists from your old program or Excel can be brought in with Import from Excel.

  • Both:

    • NIPT: needed for the VAT books.
    • Email: needed to email invoices and send payment reminders.

  • Clients only:

    • VAT (own rate, e.g. 0 % export, and the invoice note);
    • VAT book column for 0 % sales;
    • Price list, with that module.

  • Suppliers only: the default purchase type (local goods, imports, investments, farmers), which decides the purchase book columns.

8. Shops and warehouses (optional)

Stock → Locations: rename the main location and add the others. Then set each user's Works at: their sales, POS and adjustments happen there.

9. Cash and bank accounts (optional)

Cash & Bank → Accounts: one per cash register and bank account (and per currency).

  • Opening balance and date: what's in each account on the day you start. With Accounting, these become the opening entries automatically (against account 890).

  • Default account: payments by cash go to the default cash register, payments by bank or card to the default bank account. You can still pick another account per payment.

10. Exchange rates (only with Multi-currency)

Cash & Bank → Exchange rates: the day's EUR and USD rates. A EUR or USD order or payment can't be created on a day without a rate (or a rate typed by hand).

11. Opening stock

Arkon can only sell what it knows is in stock. Enter what you have on the first day in one of two ways:

  • Best: an opening purchase. Create a supplier called e.g. "Gjendja fillestare" and record one purchase with every product, its quantity and the price you paid. This gives each product its real average cost, so profit is right from the first sale. Record no payment for it.

  • Quicker: Stock → Adjustments → Other: add to stock. The cost is then the product's buy price.

  • Fastest for many products: Import from Excel → Opening stock. Fill in product, quantity and unit cost (and batches / serials when used). Arkon creates the opening purchase for you, one per location.

With Batches and serial numbers, the opening purchase is where you enter batches, expiry dates and serial numbers.

12. Opening balances (only with Accounting)

Accounting → Manual entries, dated on your start date, each against 890 Llogari e çeljes:

What you had Entry
Unpaid sales from before Arkon Dr 411 Klientët / Cr 890
Unpaid purchases from before Arkon Dr 890 / Cr 401 Furnitorët
Capital Dr 890 / Cr 101 Kapitali
Fixed assets (if not in the Fixed assets module) Dr 215 / Cr 890
Loans Dr 890 / Cr 164 Huatë
  • Cash and bank come from the accounts' opening balances (step 9).

  • Stock comes from the opening purchase (step 11). An opening purchase without a payment shows as owed to the "Gjendja fillestare" supplier. Ask your accountant to clear it with Dr 401 / Cr 890 once.

  • Check: after all opening entries, 890 should be zero.

Unpaid invoices from before Arkon: if you also want to collect them in Arkon (payment status, reminders), record them as sales or purchases dated on their original date instead of a manual entry.

3. Purchases

Before you start

  • your company (the buyer), set up in Administration → Companies
  • the supplier (supplier), with NIPT for the purchase book
  • the products, with unit and buy price
  • with Shops: the location the goods arrive at
  • with Multi-currency: the day's rate for EUR / USD purchases

This affects

  • stock: quantities go up at once
  • average cost, and so the profit of later sales
  • Unpaid to suppliers and aged payables, until paid
  • Accounting: Dr 355 + 4456 VAT / Cr 401, and the purchase book
  • the reorder list (For supply)

A purchase records stock coming in from a supplier.

Creating a purchase

  1. Open it: Purchases → New purchase. The page says "New purchase" without a number. It gets the next number (00000001, 00000002…) when the first product is saved, so abandoned pages don't leave gaps.

  2. Supplier and company: choose the Supplier (supplier) and your Company. Their details appear underneath.

  3. Add products one at a time:

    • Scan or type code: scan a barcode (or type the code and press Enter) to pick the product. Or choose it from the Product list.

    • Quantity.

    • Put on shelf (optional): the shelf where you'll store this delivery. The quantity is added to that shelf.

    • Price paid per unit: leave it empty to use the product's buy price. Fill it in when this delivery cost a different price; this is what makes profit figures accurate (see Statistics).

    • The price row shows price, unit and the values without VAT, VAT and with VAT as you type.

    • Click Add product.

  4. Done: after the first product, the supplier and company are fixed for this order.

Purchases have no stock limit: you can always buy.

After saving

  • Create invoice: makes the PDF (administrators).

  • Payments: record what you've paid the supplier (see section 6).

  • Remove: takes a wrong line off the order. To change a quantity, remove the line and add it again.

Purchase type and supplier invoice number

  • On every saved purchase: a bar with:

    • Purchase type: goods from local suppliers (default), imported goods, investment from a local supplier, imported investment, or from local farmers;

    • Supplier invoice no.: the number on the supplier's invoice.

    Click Save. The type decides the columns in the tax office's purchase book.

  • Default type per supplier (Administration → Suppliers → a supplier): e.g. "Imported goods" for a foreign supplier. New purchases from that supplier start with it.

4. Sales

Before you start

  • the client (VAT setting, price list, email)
  • stock: from purchases or opening stock; a shop sells only its own stock
  • prices: sale price, client class discounts, price lists
  • with Multi-currency: the day's rate for EUR / USD sales

This affects

  • stock goes down; the cost of goods is taken from the average cost
  • profit in reports and statistics
  • Unpaid by clients, aged receivables, reminders, until paid
  • Accounting: Dr 411 / Cr 701 + 4457 VAT, and Dr 607 / Cr 355; the sales book
  • the dashboard, statistics and budget vs actual

A sale records stock going out to a client.

Creating a sale

  1. Open it: Sales → New sale. Choose your Company and the Client.

  2. Choose a product: scan or choose it. Under Quantity you see how many are in stock (green), or Out of stock (red). Take from shelf (only when shelves are switched on). This list shows each shelf holding the product with its quantity, e.g. A1 (2 cope), A2 (3 cope), plus Not on a shelf for stock that isn't on any shelf:

    • One place: it's selected automatically.
    • Several places: choose where to take it from.
    • More than one shelf needed: add the product twice, e.g. 2 from A1 and 3 from A2.

  3. Read the price row:

    Column Meaning
    Price The product's sale price
    Unit pcs, kg…
    Discount The client's discount for this product's class (0 % if none)
    Unit price Price after the discount
    Value (no VAT) / VAT / Value (with VAT) For the quantity typed

  4. Messages under the price row:

    • "Not enough stock…" (red): you typed more than is in stock. Add product is disabled until you lower the quantity. The server checks this again, so it can't be bypassed.

    • "After this, only … will remain in stock, below the quantity limit" (amber): only a warning. You can still add the line.

  5. Add product.

Cost of each sale line

When a sale line is saved, Arkon also saves the product's average purchase cost at that moment. You don't see this on the order, but Statistics uses it for profit. See section 11.

After saving

Payments, Create invoice and Remove work as described for purchases. Removing a sale line puts its quantity back on the shelf it came from.

Pick list: the sale page has Pick list (a printable page) and Pick list PDF. Give it to the person collecting the goods (see Shelves and pick lists).

Returns (credit notes and return notes)

Returns are recorded on the order they belong to, so everything is corrected at once. They work the same on both sides:

  • Sales: goods a client brings back. Stock goes up, and a credit note is created.

  • Purchases: goods you send back to the supplier (broken, wrong item…). Stock goes down, and a return note is created. You can only return what's still in stock.

Steps:

  1. Enter the quantities: open the sale or purchase, scroll to Returns and enter how many of each item.

  2. Choose a reason:

    • damaged or broken;
    • defective, not working;
    • wrong item;
    • no longer needed;
    • quality not as expected;
    • other.

    Add a note if useful. Reasons feed the return statistics.

  3. Shelf (only if shelves are on): where the goods go back to, or are taken from. By default it's the shelf they came from.

  4. Record: click Record return.

What happens:

  • The order: gets minus lines marked Return, so its total goes down.

  • Every total follows: reports, statistics, profit, the Home figures, unpaid amounts and the list totals.

  • The PDF: the credit note or return note (e.g. 00000012-1) is created, linked on the order and listed under Invoices.

  • Refunds:

    • Sales: if the client had already paid, the sale shows Refund due. Record the money with Record refund in Payments.

    • Purchases: if you had already paid the supplier, the purchase shows Refund due. Record the money with Record refund received when the supplier pays it back.

Returns lists: all returns are listed under Sales → Sales returns and Purchases → Returns to suppliers. Each shows the date, number, client or supplier, products, units, value, reason, note and PDF. You can search them and filter by date.

To undo a return, remove its Return lines from the order. A line that was partly returned can't be removed until its return lines are removed.

The stock adjustments in section 7 are still there for stock changes that don't belong to a sale or purchase in Arkon.

VAT per client and per sale

Normally every sale uses the VAT rate in Settings (e.g. 20%). Two options change that when needed:

  • Per client (Administration → Clients → a client → VAT): enter the client's own rate (e.g. 0) and the note to print on their invoices (e.g. the exemption wording your accountant gives you). Every new sale and offer for that client uses it. Leave it empty for the normal rate.

  • Per sale or offer: when the client has no VAT of its own, the order page shows a VAT bar.

    • Change for this sale: type a rate and a note, then Save. All lines are recalculated.

    • Back to normal: returns the order to the normal rate.

    • Locked after payment: VAT can be changed only before the first payment is recorded.

Each order keeps the rate it was made with, so changing a client's VAT later doesn't change old invoices. Purchases always use the normal rate. The VAT books, accounting and reports use each line's actual VAT.

Which rate and wording apply (0% export of goods, services to foreign businesses, exemptions) depends on your case. Ask your accountant.

5. Offers

Before you start

  • the client and products, as for a sale
  • Offers valid for (days) in Settings: the expiry date

This affects

  • nothing in stock or accounting: an offer is only a proposal
  • when turned into a sale: everything a sale affects (stock checked then)
  • the dashboard's open offers

An offer is a price quotation. It does not change stock and is not a sale until you turn it into one.

Creating an offer

  1. Open it: Offers → New offer. Choose your Company.

  2. Choose who it's for:

    • An existing client: pick from the list. Their class discounts apply automatically.

    • Someone new: tick New client (not registered yet), then:

      • type their name and fill in TIN, phone, address and email (optional, printed on the offer);

      • optionally enter a Discount % by hand; it applies to every line;

      • if the name you type is already a client, a hint tells you to choose it from the list instead.

  3. Add products, as on a sale. The stock check also applies to offers, so you can't offer more than you have.

Offer status

Each saved offer has an Offer status panel:

Status Meaning
Open Waiting for the client's answer
Accepted The client said yes (set by you)
Rejected The client said no (set by you)
Expired Shown automatically when an Open offer passes its Valid until date
Converted The offer was turned into a sale; a link to the sale is shown

Change Status or Valid until and click Save.

Turn into sale

Turn into sale creates a new sale from the offer in one step:

  • Lines and prices: every line is copied with the prices that were offered, even if prices changed since.

  • Stock check first: stock is checked for every product. If anything is short, nothing is created and a message lists what's missing.

  • New clients: if the offer was for a new client, they're registered as a client automatically, using the details from the offer. This needs an administrator.

  • Afterwards: the offer becomes Converted with a link to the sale, the sale opens, and stock is reduced.

Statistics shows how many offers turn into sales (see section 11).

7. Returns and stock adjustments

Before you start

  • for a return: the original sale or purchase (you return its lines)
  • for an adjustment: the product, and with Shops the location

This affects

  • stock (back in, or out)
  • a return: a credit note / return note, and the order's balance (a refund may be due)
  • Accounting and the VAT books (returns are negative)
  • Statistics → Returns (reasons, products, clients)

Where: Administration → Returns and stock adjustments (also linked from the warehouse report).

What it's for: changing stock without a purchase or sale. Use it whenever the physical stock changes for any other reason, or when the recorded stock is simply wrong.

The reasons

Choose the reason that matches what happened. The reason decides whether stock goes up or down; you always type a positive quantity.

Reason Stock Use it when Example
Returned by a client + A client brings goods back A client returns 2 faulty switches from sale 00000012
Returned to a supplier − You send goods back to a supplier You return 10 m of damaged cable to the supplier
Damaged − Goods broke or became unsellable in your warehouse A box of bulbs was dropped; 6 broke
Lost or stolen − Goods are missing 1 drill disappeared from the shop
Stock count: more than recorded + A physical count finds more than Arkon shows Arkon says 40, the shelf has 42: add 2
Stock count: less than recorded − A physical count finds fewer than Arkon shows Arkon says 40, the shelf has 37: remove 3
Other: add to stock + Any other increase A free sample from a supplier
Other: remove from stock − Any other decrease Goods taken for your own use

Recording an adjustment

  1. Choose the product: pick it from the list (its unit is shown).

  2. Choose the reason: the list is split into Adds to stock and Removes from stock.

  3. Enter the quantity: always positive.

  4. Shelf (optional): for removals, the shelf the goods come off; for additions, the shelf they go onto. Use it whenever the product is on shelves, so the shelves stay accurate.

  5. Reference (optional): e.g. the sale or purchase number it relates to. Recommended for returns.

  6. Note (optional): what happened.

  7. Save adjustment.

Rules:

  • Never below zero: removing more than is in stock is refused, with a message saying how many are actually in stock.

  • Signs in the list: green + for additions, red − for removals.

  • Search: live, by product, reference or note.

Barcode labels

Stock → Barcode labels prints labels with the product name, price and barcode.

  1. Choose: search for products and type how many labels each needs.

  2. Size: a roll of 50×30, 40×25 or 58×40 mm for a label printer, or A4 sheets of 3×8 labels.

  3. Price: with VAT, without VAT, or none.

  4. Print: Print labels opens a page ready to print. On a label printer, set the printer's paper to the same size.

  • The barcode is the product code: 13-digit codes print as EAN-13, others as Code 128. Scanning one on an order page or the POS picks the product.

  • No code, no barcode: products without a code get a label with no barcode. Give them a code first (Stock → Products), e.g. your own P0001 numbering.

Undo

Undo next to an adjustment cancels it, and stock changes back:

  • Undoing a removal adds the stock back.
  • Undoing an addition removes it again. This is refused if that stock has meanwhile been sold, because stock would go negative.

Every adjustment and undo is recorded in the audit log.

Returns and money

A return changes stock only; it doesn't change any order or payment. If the client gets money back:

  • Before they paid: record a smaller payment, so the invoice stays partly open. Then change the due date or settle it as agreed.

  • After they paid: give the refund outside Arkon, and write it in the adjustment's Note for reference.

The original sale stays unchanged, so its invoice and sales figures still show the original sale.

Worked example: a client returns goods

  1. The sale: on sale 00000012, Beni Shehu bought 12 automatic switches.
  2. The return: he returns 2 faulty ones.

  3. In Arkon: add an adjustment with product Automatic switch 16A, reason Returned by a client, quantity 2, reference 00000012, note "faulty, swapped next week".

  4. Result: stock goes up by 2, and the warehouse report shows +2 in the Adjusted column.

  5. If the switches can't be resold: add a second adjustment with reason Damaged, quantity 2. Stock goes back down, and the record shows exactly what happened.

Worked example: a stock count

  1. Count the warehouse: compare each product's real quantity with Administration → Products (the "In stock" column) or the warehouse report.

  2. Where the shelf has more: add Stock count: more than recorded with the difference.

  3. Where the shelf has less: add Stock count: less than recorded with the difference.

  4. Reference: use something like "Count 31/12/2026" on all of them, so you can find them together later with the search box.

8. How stock is counted

Before you start

  • product names (stock is counted by name)

This affects

  • every sale check ("not enough stock")
  • reports, the reorder list, the dashboard's To restock

For each product:

In stock = everything purchased
         − everything sold
         + adjustments that add (returns from clients, stock count +, other +)
         − adjustments that remove (returns to suppliers, damage, loss, stock count −, other −)
  • Offers never change stock.

  • The same figure everywhere: this number is used by the sale and offer stock check, the In stock column in Products, the warehouse report and To restock on Home.

  • Counted by product name: if you rename a product, its stock count starts again from zero, because past orders keep the old name. Rename only products without stock, or record a stock adjustment after renaming.

8b. Shelves and pick lists

Before you start

  • the Shelves setting switched on, and the shelves created
  • stock placed on shelves

This affects

  • sales: choosing the shelf to take from
  • pick lists for collecting goods

Where: Administration → Shelves.

Shelves are optional and switched off by default. Turn them on in Administration → Settings → Shelves (Use shelves). When they're off:

  • Hidden: shelf fields, shelf columns and the Shelves menu item disappear.

  • Never limiting: shelves no longer affect purchases, sales or adjustments.

  • Kept: your shelf data stays, so you can switch them back on any time.

Shelves record where your stock physically is, so whoever collects a sale knows exactly where to go. They don't change how much you have; that still comes from purchases, sales and adjustments (section 8).

How it works

  • Spread over shelves: a product's stock can be on several shelves, e.g. Cable 3x2.5 → A1: 20 m, B2: 30 m.

  • Not on a shelf: stock that isn't on any shelf. All your existing stock starts here, so everything keeps working before you set up shelves.

Not on a shelf = total stock − everything on shelves

  • Always consistent: shelves can never hold more than the total. You can only take from a shelf what's on it.

Setting up

  1. Add your shelves: a code (A1, A2, B1…, unique) and an optional description (e.g. "Back room, top").

  2. Place existing stock: the Not on a shelf yet box lists products with unplaced stock. Use Place or move stock: choose the product and quantity, From: Not on a shelf, To: the shelf, then Move.

  3. Check: each shelf card lists its products, codes and quantities. The Shelves column in Administration → Products shows e.g. A1: 2, A2: 3 for each product.

Place or move stock

From To Result
Not on a shelf A shelf Places stock on the shelf
A shelf Another shelf Moves it between shelves
A shelf Not on a shelf Takes it off the shelf (it stays in stock)

You can't move more than the source holds. Every move is recorded in the audit log.

Purchases, sales and adjustments

Action Shelf effect
Purchase with Put on shelf Adds the quantity to that shelf
Purchase without a shelf Stock arrives as not on a shelf
Sale with Take from shelf Removes the quantity from that shelf (refused if the shelf has less)
Sale from Not on a shelf Allowed for stock that isn't on a shelf; otherwise Arkon asks you to choose the shelf
Removing a sale line or deleting a sale Puts the quantity back on its shelf
Removing a purchase line or deleting a purchase Takes the quantity back off its shelf
Stock adjustment with a shelf Removals come off it, additions go onto it; Undo reverses it
Offer turned into a sale Arkon picks: stock not on a shelf first, otherwise the one shelf with enough. If the stock is split over several shelves, it asks you to create the sale by hand
Deleting a shelf Its stock isn't lost; it becomes not on a shelf

Pick lists

On a saved sale, click Pick list (opens a page to print) or Pick list PDF. It shows:

  • the sale number, client, date, your company, and who prepared it;

  • each line with code, product, quantity and the shelf, shown large on an amber background so it's easy to read in the warehouse;

  • a tick box per line;

  • signature lines for Picked by, Checked by and Date and time.

A product taken from two shelves appears as two lines.

Worked example

  1. Receive goods: a purchase of 50 m cable is put on shelf A1. A1 now holds 50 m.

  2. Rearrange: you move 30 m from A1 to B2. Now A1 holds 20 m and B2 holds 30 m.

  3. Sell from one shelf: a sale of 25 m. Choose B2 (30 m); B2 then holds 5 m.

  4. Sell from two shelves: a client wants 22 m. Add 20 m from A1 and 2 m from B2 as two lines.

  5. Collect: print the Pick list. The warehouse sees A1 → 20 m and B2 → 2 m, ticks both off, and signs.

Before you start

  • the module switched on (Settings → Modules)

This affects

  • every payment lands in an account
  • Accounting (512 bank, 531 cash, and the account of each money in / out category)
  • the cash-flow forecast, the dashboard's cash balances
  • Payroll and Tax due when paid through it

Where: the Arka & Banka (Cash & Bank) tab, for administrators. It's an optional module, off by default: switch it on in Settings → Modules. While it's off, the tab, pages and the payment Account field are hidden, but payments are still recorded in the background, so switching it on later shows the full history.

It shows how much money you have in each cash register and bank account, and every entry and exit.

Accounts

  • Defaults: two accounts exist from the start, Arka (cash) and Banka (bank).

  • Accounts page: add more, e.g. a second bank, with the bank name and IBAN. Each account has:

    • an opening balance and opening date (what was in it when you started using Arkon);

    • Active (inactive accounts are hidden from forms);

    • Default (the account payments go to automatically).

  • Deleting: an account with movements can't be deleted; make it inactive instead.

Payments go in automatically

When you record a payment or refund on a sale or purchase, it becomes a movement:

Payment Account Direction
Client pays a sale Cash → Arka, Bank transfer or Card → Banka In
You pay a purchase Same Out
Refund to a client Same Out
Refund from a supplier Same In

  • Choosing the account: the payment form has an Account field that follows the method. You can choose a different account.

  • Deleting: deleting a payment (or its order) removes the movement too.

  • Older payments: those recorded before this feature were carried over (cash to Arka, others to Banka).

Other money in and out

Use + Money in / − Money out for anything that isn't a sale or purchase payment:

  • In: capital, loan received, other income.
  • Out:
    • rent, salaries, utilities, transport and fuel;
    • taxes, bank fees;
    • owner withdrawal, loan repayment;
    • other expenses.

Enter the account, date, category, amount, a description and an optional reference (e.g. a receipt number).

Transfers

Transfer moves money between your own accounts, e.g. depositing the day's cash into the bank: Arka goes down, Banka goes up. Transfers are not income or expense, so the cash flow report leaves them out.

Reconcile (barazo gjendjen)

  1. Count: count the cash in the till, or look at the bank statement.

  2. Enter the real balance: in Reconcile, choose the account, the date and the balance you counted (Arkon shows its own figure next to each account).

  3. Correction: if they differ, Arkon records the difference as a Correction (+/−), so its balance matches reality. Investigate big differences.

Import a bank statement

Cash & Bank → Import statement. Instead of typing every bank movement, read the bank's statement.

  1. Download: from your e-banking, download the statement as CSV (or save the Excel as CSV).

  2. Upload: choose the bank account and the file, then Read the statement.

  3. Columns: Arkon recognises the date, description and amount (or debit / credit) columns, in Albanian or English, with ; , or tab separators and numbers like 1.234,56. If a column is wrong, pick the right one at the top and click Read again.

  4. Review each line: it shows what Arkon found:

    Found Meaning Suggested
    Already in Arkon a movement with the same amount and direction within 5 days Mark as matched
    Pays Sale / Purchase … the amount equals what an unpaid invoice still owes (a name or number in the text is preferred) Record the payment
    Not in Arkon nothing matches Add as money in / out, with a category (bank fees, rent…)
    Imported before this exact line was imported already nothing

  5. Save: change any action you want (or Skip a line), then Save. Everything is saved together.

  • No duplicates: each saved line remembers its statement line, so importing the same (or an overlapping) statement again never doubles anything.

  • Then reconcile: after the import, Reconcile the account with the statement's closing balance. It should match.

Money in and money out (the register)

Cash & Bank → + Money in and − Money out work like a register book: you type many lines one after another instead of one form at a time.

  1. Choose the account at the top. Its balance now is shown.

  2. Type the rows. Each row has a date, description, category, amount, reference no. and (with cost centres) a cost centre. Money in shows money-in categories (capital, other income…); Money out shows money-out ones (rent, salaries, bank fees…).

    • Tab moves to the next field.
    • Enter adds a new row with the same date and category.

  3. Check as you go:

    • Balance after each row updates as you type.

  4. Save all. Empty rows are skipped; if a row is incomplete, nothing is saved and the row numbers are shown.

Overview, movements and cash flow

  • Overview:

    • the balance of cash, bank and the total;
    • one card per account;
    • this month's in, out and net;
    • the latest movements.

    Click an account card to see its movements.

  • Movements: every entry and exit, filtered by account, category, dates and search. With one account chosen, it shows the opening balance and the balance after each movement, like a bank statement. It has totals and Export to Excel. Hand-entered movements and transfers can be deleted here; payments are deleted on their order.

  • Cash flow: money in and out by category for any period, the net result, and a chart per day (or month for long periods).

  • Analytics (Analitika): comparisons.

    • A month vs the previous month, or vs the same month last year. For the current month it compares the same number of days, e.g. 1–20 October vs 1–20 September.

    • This year so far vs the same days last year.

    • Change %: in, out and net each show the change, in green when it's good (more in, less out, higher net) and red when it's not.

    • Per category: each category's amount in both periods and the difference.

    • Charts for the last 12 months:

      • money in and out next to the same months a year earlier;
      • the net result per month (with last year's line);
      • the total balance at each month end.

### Production (Prodhimi)

  1. Bill of materials: choose the product you make and add its components with the quantity needed for one unit. The page shows what one unit costs to make at today's average costs.

  2. Production order: product and quantity. The components are listed with their costs.

  3. Make it: Arkon checks the components are in stock, then:

    • the components leave stock (Used in production);
    • the finished product enters stock (Produced);
    • the production cost per unit is saved and feeds the product's average cost, so profit on its sales is right.

  4. Reverse undoes it, as long as the produced goods are still in stock. Cancel removes a draft.

If shelves are on, components are taken from stock that isn't on a shelf.

Tax due (Detyrimet tatimore)

For each of the last 12 months:

  • VAT: VAT on sales − VAT on purchases (returns included). A negative amount is a credit.

  • Payroll taxes (with Payroll on): income tax + employee and employer contributions of approved payrolls.

  • Columns: the due date (VAT on the 14th, payroll on the 20th of the next month, both editable), what's paid, what's open, and the status: due, overdue, paid, credit, or month running.

Record a tax payment: choose the tax, the month, the amount and the date. With Cash & Bank on, choose the account and it's recorded as Taxes.

These figures help you prepare; your accountant files the returns.

VAT books: the sales book is a tab in Sales and the purchase book a tab in Purchases (they need access to VAT books).

Exchange rate adjustment (year end)

Accounting → Exchange rate adjustment (with Multi-currency).

At the end of the year, what is still held or owed in EUR / USD must be valued at that day's rate. Arkon lists:

Item In the books At the rate
each EUR / USD cash or bank account its lek value so far balance × the rate
each EUR / USD invoice still open on that date (client owes, or we owe a supplier) the open amount at the invoice's rate open amount × the rate

The difference: an exchange gain (769) or loss (669). For example, a client owing 1,000 EUR booked at 98.50 is worth 99,200 at 99.20, a 700 gain. A supplier we owe 2,000 EUR is a 1,400 loss at the same rise, because we owe more lek.

  1. Rates: choose the date (e.g. 31.12). The day's rates are filled in; change them if needed, then Calculate.

  2. Check the items and the net gain / loss.

  3. Post the adjustment: one journal entry.

    • Reverse on the next day (recommended) adds the opposite entry on 1 January. When an invoice is later paid, Arkon books its whole exchange difference against the invoice's own rate; without the reversal, the year-end part would be counted twice.

  4. Undo: a date can be adjusted only once. Undo removes that date's adjustment and reversal.

The opening balance of a EUR / USD bank account now enters the journal in lek at the rate of its opening date. Run Update automatic entries once if you have such accounts.

8i. Multi-currency (EUR, USD)

Before you start

  • the module switched on
  • the day's exchange rates (or a rate typed on the order)
  • for EUR / USD money: cash or bank accounts in that currency

This affects

  • invoices in the order's currency, with the ALL summary
  • payments and exchange differences (769 / 669)
  • everything else stays in ALL (reports, accounting, VAT books)

Optional module, off by default (Settings → Modules → Multi-currency).

The base currency (Settings, usually ALL) stays the currency of every report, the unpaid lists, statistics, profit, accounting and the VAT books. Every amount is also kept in the base currency, so those all keep working as before.

Exchange rates

Administration → Exchange rates: enter the rate of the day, e.g. 1 EUR = 98.50 ALL and 1 USD = 91.20 ALL (the Bank of Albania rate). Arkon uses the latest rate on or before each date. Without a rate, it asks you to enter one first.

Orders in EUR or USD

  1. Choose the currency: on a new sale, purchase or offer, choose EUR or USD before adding the first product. Today's rate is used. When choosing EUR or USD, a Rate field appears with today's rate; type another rate if you prefer.

  2. The currency bar: the order page then shows the currency, the rate, and the total in that currency and in ALL. While nothing is paid you can change the rate there.

    • Purchases: the price paid per unit is typed in EUR or USD; stock cost and profit stay in ALL.

    • Sales and offers: priced from the product's ALL price (and price lists), converted at the order's rate.

  3. Payments: typed in the order's currency, and they count at the order's rate, so the balance always adds up.

  4. Invoices and credit notes: lines and totals are in the order's currency. The header shows the currency and rate, and a box at the bottom gives the summary in ALL: taxable supplies, VAT-free supplies, total VAT and total, as Albanian invoices show them.

  5. Offers: an offer in EUR becomes a sale in EUR at the same rate.

Exchange differences (automatic)

A payment in EUR or USD has a Rate on payment day field, filled in from the daily rates. Type a different rate if your bank used another one.

  • Clearing the invoice: the payment clears the invoice at the order's rate, so the invoice shows as fully paid.

  • Cash & Bank gets the real amount: EUR in a EUR account, or the real lek in an ALL account.

  • The difference is the exchange gain or loss. It shows on the payment, and Accounting books it to 769 Exchange gains or 669 Exchange losses.

Example: an invoice of 1,000 EUR at 98.50 = 98,500 ALL, paid at 99.20 = 99,200 ALL, gives a 700 ALL gain. Paying a supplier after the euro rose gives a loss, because the euros cost you more lek.

Why it matters even when you keep the euros: your books are in lek. The 1,000 EUR you received is worth 99,200 ALL, but it cleared a debt booked at 98,500 ALL. Exchanging the euros later is a separate transfer, whose difference is booked to 769 / 669 too.

Reports → Exchange differences lists every payment with a difference, with the gains, losses and net for a period.

Cash and bank accounts in EUR or USD

  • Account currency: in Cash & Bank → Accounts, choose the account's currency. Its opening balance, entries and reconciling are in that currency, and its card shows the balance in EUR or USD with the ALL equivalent. Overview totals are converted to ALL at today's rate.

  • Exchanging money: use a Transfer between accounts in different currencies, entering both the amount sent and the amount received (e.g. 100 EUR out, 9,850 ALL in). In accounting, any lek difference is booked as an exchange gain or loss.

  • Changing an account's currency: only possible before it has movements.

  1. Pick the products: in the For supply list, tick the products to reorder. To order is pre-filled with enough to get back above the limit; change it if you like.

  2. Create it: choose the supplier and your company, then click Create purchase order.

  3. Send it: the purchase order (e.g. PO-00001) has a PDF, plus an Email to the supplier panel when email is on. While it's open, you can change quantities (0 removes a product) or add a note.

  4. Receive it: when the goods arrive, click Goods received. Arkon creates the real purchase with these products and prices, and only now does stock go up. Or Cancel order if it's no longer needed.

Headline figures

Figure How it's calculated
Sales (with VAT) Sum of sale lines in the period; also number of orders and items
Purchases (with VAT) Sum of purchase lines in the period
Estimated gross profit Sales without VAT − cost of the goods sold. The margin % is profit ÷ sales without VAT
Average sale Sales with VAT ÷ number of sales
Offers turned into sales Offers converted ÷ offers made in the period

How profit uses real costs. Each sale line stores the product's average purchase cost when it was sold. That's the total value of all its purchases divided by the total quantity purchased.

Purchase Quantity Price paid
January 100 180
March 100 200

The average cost is (100 × 180 + 100 × 200) ÷ 200 = 190. A sale of 10 units at 250 then earns (250 − 190) × 10 = 600 profit.

Sales made before costs were stored use the product's current buy price.

Backup and restore

Where: Administration → Backup and restore.

  • Export backup: saves everything (sales, purchases, offers, payments, products, shelves, users, settings, audit log, invoice PDFs and logo) into one .zip file. In Chrome, Edge and Brave a Save as window asks where to put it; other browsers save it to Downloads. Keep copies on another disk or in cloud storage.

  • Restore: choose a backup file, tick the confirmation box, and click Restore backup. All current data is replaced by the backup's:

    • Safety copy first: Arkon saves a copy of the current data in the backups folder of the data directory.

    • All or nothing: the data is replaced in one step; if anything goes wrong, nothing changes.

    • Log in again: everyone is logged out afterwards. Users and passwords are now the ones from the backup.

    • Same version only: a backup can only be restored into the same Arkon version it came from. The page says so if they differ.

Automatic backups

On the Backup and restore page, Automatic backups saves a backup by itself, so a lost or broken computer doesn't take your data with it.

Setting Choices
Folder where to save: another disk, a USB drive, a network share, or a folder synced by OneDrive / Google Drive / Dropbox (a path on the computer running Arkon)
When once a day at a time (e.g. 02:00), or every 1–12 hours
What full (data + invoice PDFs + logo), or data only (smaller)
Files keep dated copies (Arkon-backup_2026-10-22_02-00.zip…), or one file replaced each time (Arkon-backup-latest.zip)

  • Back up now makes one immediately. Use it to test the folder.

  • Last result: the page shows the last backup (OK in green, or the error in red) and when the next one runs. A failure is also in the audit log.

  • If the computer was off at the backup time, the backup runs as soon as Arkon is running again.

  • Retention is safe: it only ever deletes Arkon's own dated backup files in that folder, never other files and never the newest backup.

  • Restore: a backup restores with Restore a backup, like a manual one.

  • Recommended: daily, full, dated copies, keep the last 14, in a cloud-synced folder.

Audit log

A record of who did what, and when. It includes:

  • logins;
  • order lines added and removed, and orders deleted;
  • invoices created and deleted;
  • payments added and deleted, and due dates changed;
  • offer status changes and offers turned into sales;
  • stock adjustments and undos;

  • products, suppliers, clients, companies and users created, changed or deleted;

  • settings changes and password changes.

Searching: live, by user, action, order number, product or any detail, and by date range.

Clear all logs deletes every entry, after confirmation. The clearing itself becomes the first entry of the new log, recording who cleared it, when, and how many entries were deleted.

Every day

  1. Morning:

    • Open the Dashboard: overdue invoices, low stock, taxes due.
    • The bell lists what needs attention.

  2. Sell and buy: record sales (or use the POS) and purchases as they happen, not at the end of the day. Stock and debts are then always right.

  3. Payments:

    • Record every payment on its order the day the money arrives or leaves.

    • With Cash & Bank, choose the right account.

  4. Evening:

    • With Cash & Bank: Reconcile the cash register against what's in the drawer.

    • A difference is recorded as a correction, so you see it rather than hiding it.

At the end of each month

  1. Check purchases: every supplier invoice of the month is recorded, with the supplier invoice number and the right purchase type.

  2. Check unpaid: look at Unpaid and the aged receivables, and send reminders.

  3. Bank statement: with Cash & Bank, Import statement (the bank's CSV), then Reconcile each bank account with the statement's balance.

  4. Payroll: check Reports → Commissions and add commissions to the salespeople's gross. Create the month's payroll run, check it, approve it, and mark it paid when salaries leave the bank. Export the Listëpagesa.

  5. Accounting:

    • Update automatic entries for the month.
    • Check the trial balance. Debits and credits must be equal.
    • Look at any account that looks wrong in the general ledger.

  6. VAT:

    • Export the sales book and purchase book (Excel – Tatimet) for your accountant.

    • Check Tax due for VAT, TAP and contributions.

  7. Lock the month: once your accountant has filed, set the period lock to the last day of the month. Nobody can then change that month by mistake.

At the end of the year

  1. Stock count: do a stock count (per location with Shops) on the last day, and apply the corrections.

  2. Fixed assets: check the year's depreciation is posted.

  3. Update entries: run Update automatic entries for the whole year, and check the trial balance.

  4. Official statements: open Accounting → Official statements.

    • Clear every warning (accounts without a line).
    • Export the Excel (official format) for your accountant.

  5. Close the year: after the statements are approved, close the year (Accounting → Statements → year-end closing). This moves the year's result to retained earnings.

  6. Lock and back up: set the period lock to 31 December, and export a backup and keep it safe.

A new employee starts

  1. User account: Administration → Users → Add user: username, password, role.

  2. Access: only what they need. For example, Full on Sales but None on Payments received, or View on payroll settings.

  3. Records this user sees: usually Only their own for sales staff.

  4. Works at: their shop (with Shops and warehouses).

  5. Payroll: add them as an employee, with gross salary, tax code (001/002) and whether they filed the yearly declaration. Use Calculate gross from net if you agreed a net salary.

  6. Ask them to sign in and, if it's required, set up two-step login.

An employee leaves

  1. Hand over records: on their user page, so clients and open offers stay followed up by someone.

  2. End payroll: set their end date on the employee, so payroll runs stop including them.

  3. Switch off the account: deactivate the user (or change the password). Their past work stays in the records and the audit log.

Opening a new shop

  1. Location: Stock → Locations → Add a location (code, name, address).

  2. Cash register: with Cash & Bank, add one for the shop.

  3. Goods: transfer goods from the warehouse to the new shop.
  4. Staff: set the shop staff's Works at to the new shop.
  5. Check: Stock by location shows the new shop's stock.

Starting with a business that already runs

Follow chapter 2, steps 9–12:

  • Cash and bank: opening balances on each account.
  • Opening stock: an opening purchase, at what you paid.
  • Old debts, capital and assets: opening entries against 890.
  • Check: check 890 = 0 in the trial balance.

From then on, record everything in Arkon.
